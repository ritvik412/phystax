# Demo video: 3 minutes, narrated

Screens: the live page (`/live` on the lab server), then the replay site.
About 150 words a minute; ~450 words. Record segments separately, cut together.

**0:00–0:12 · Hook** (you, or the live page)
[Your own line.] "Every team here uses SAM to measure the world. We measured
SAM, and asked whether an AI scientist that sees only through it can find a law
it was never told, and commit to predictions it can't take back."

**0:12–0:45 · Your experiment, live** (/live)
"I toss a ball. SAM 3.1 tracks it from one tap; that box is SAM. Muse Spark sees
the first half of the flight, decides what kind of motion this is, and seals a
prediction for the second half: hashed before those frames are read. Now
reality: orange is the sealed prediction, grey is what the ball did. Error:
[read the number]."

**0:45–1:10 · A universe nobody has seen** (site, episode steps)
"Real objects follow textbook laws, so we also built universes with made-up
force laws. The agent designs its own experiments, seals a law, and the universe
grades it on conditions it never tried."

**1:10–1:35 · Where the error comes from, and what the law is worth** (bars)
"Because we know the truth, every error splits into camera, experiments and
reasoning. The camera costs almost nothing; the error is reasoning. And when a
fresh forecaster gets the agent's law, its predictions improve by [P2 credit],
but the true law would improve them by [P2 gap] more."

**1:35–1:55 · SAM 3.1 as an instrument** (naming table, coverage rows)
"Sub-pixel away from edges, five to eight times worse at them; names fail where
a tap never does; and correlated tracking errors make textbook uncertainty
overconfident."

**1:55–2:35 · An open problem from Meta's research** (P1 chart and table)
"Meta's paper proves that an ellipsoid through n random points in d dimensions
exists below d²/4 and fails above, and leaves the edge open. We measured the
edge, and before computing larger dimensions we sealed four predictions,
including a conjecture from conic geometry: the edge sits at d(d+1)/4. Here is
how each one did. [Read the result honestly: which predictor won at d = 30, 36,
44.] Evidence, not a proof."

**2:35–2:50 · Real lab footage** (pendulum)
"And on seven real pendulum clips from the IRIS benchmark, the damping is never
the exponential the benchmark assumes."

**2:50–3:00 · Close** ("Check it yourself")
"Every claim here was sealed in a ledger before it was tested. Built with SAM 3.1
and Muse Spark. For AI scientists, the camera isn't the bottleneck. Reasoning is."

## Recording checklist

- Live: camera propped still, plain wall, good light, toss across the frame,
  tap the ball just after release. Three takes; keep the best.
- Browser 1920×1080, zoom 110%, notifications off. Final cut ≤ 3:00.
