# Demo video: 3:00, narrated live while clicking (OBS)

About 440 words at a calm pace. [Brackets] are what to show. Read from your phone.

**0:00–0:15 · The hook** [Space: top of the site]
"[Your own sentence: why you built this.] Every team in this hackathon uses
SAM to measure the world. We asked a different question: if an AI scientist
can only see the world through SAM, can it still discover how the world works,
and will it commit to predictions it can't take back?"

**0:15–0:40 · The idea** [scroll: pipeline, then the episode player]
"Here's the loop. A universe with a hidden law. A camera. SAM 3.1 turns video
into measurements. Muse Spark designs experiments and fits laws. Then it seals
a prediction, locked with a hash, before the test even exists, and reality
grades it. No model grades another model. [click Sealed prediction, then
verify] This is the seal: the time, and a proof anyone can check."

**0:40–1:05 · Live** [local /live: play your ball run]
"It also works on real footage. I tossed a ball at my webcam and tapped it once.
That box is SAM. Muse Spark saw the first half, called it free flight, and sealed
the second half. Orange is what it predicted, grey is what happened. Right law,
about twenty percent off: honest, and not yet a pass."

**1:05–1:15 · The lab** [local /lab: click Run once]
"And this is the simulator lab, where anyone picks a universe, a camera quality
and a scientist, and runs the whole experiment."

**1:15–1:40 · What we learned** [Space: decomposition bars, credit bars]
"Because we know the truth, we can split every mistake into three parts: the
camera, the experiments, the reasoning. The camera costs about three percent.
The rest is reasoning: the agent picks a cleaner law than its data supports.
But its law still carries real physics: a second forecaster given it goes from
fifty percent error to nineteen."

**1:40–2:00 · SAM as an instrument** [naming table, coverage rows]
"We also measured SAM itself: sub-pixel in the middle of the frame, five to
eight times worse at the edges, and it often misses an object you name but
never one you tap."

**2:00–2:35 · Open problems** [Open problem section: equations, table; then README table]
"Meta's research post lists open problems. One is about fitting an ellipsoid
through random points: a recent paper by Arslan and Muse Spark proves exactly
where it stops working, but not what happens at the edge itself. We measured that
edge, and sealed four predictions before computing bigger cases. My own guess
was wrong all three times. Muse Spark's landed inside the measured range every
time. The second problem comes from a CMU paper: does an AI's explanation
actually help someone predict? Here, it does. Both sources are linked in the
README."

**2:35–2:50 · Real lab footage** [pendulum section]
"On seven real pendulum videos from the IRIS benchmark, the damping isn't the
shape the benchmark assumes."

**2:50–3:00 · Why it matters** [back to the top]
"Any lab, robot or world model that measures through a vision model inherits
its errors. This shows where they go, and builds AI scientists that commit
before they're checked. The camera isn't the bottleneck. Reasoning is."

## Before recording

- Clean browser window, no other tabs, bookmarks bar hidden; Focus mode on.
- Never show a terminal or `.env`.
- Two or three full takes; keep the best. Stay under 3:00.
